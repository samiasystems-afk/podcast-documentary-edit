# Directed podcast editing

These are Samia choices informed by [the source study](doac-source-study.md), not a DOAC preset. Current user decisions take precedence.

## Paper edit

Read the whole episode transcript for context and inspect selected media. Tag tension, example, lesson, uncertainty and emotional change. Write **output range | source file/in/out | exact words | story job | shot | text | score/SFX action | later payoff**. Count actual clips and breaths. Never write dialogue and imply the guest said it.

Possible arcs: expert problem → unexpected explanation → example → practical lesson → unresolved decision; personal consequence → context → conflict → change → unanswered question. The trailer needs an insight of its own. Avoid withholding all value or assembling unrelated dramatic statements.

For a roughly 60–90s requested sequence, start immediately with understandable tension, establish context, deliver a useful insight, turn the emotional direction, and end on a specific question answered later. Shorten rather than pad. A broad future-impact question usually needs a more specific preceding stake. Keep identification brief and avoid a long repeated introduction after the teaser.

## Scene-integrated typography

Choose exact installed/licensed font files; record family, weight, case, tracking and hierarchy. Normally one purposeful display family and one support family are enough. Never silently substitute Arial or a system default. Our study did not establish DOAC's exact fonts; do not label lookalikes as theirs.

Yussef's latest feedback requires consistency with his actual YouTube channel. Retrieve the owner's logo, banner and relevant thumbnails before choosing a palette or typography. Use verified brand colours purposefully, even when an earlier generic treatment used a similar hue. For AI Cross Fields use black, white and sampled amber, the original channel logo, and heavy condensed inclined headings. See [brand reference](ai-cross-fields-brand.md). Exact raster-artwork fonts remain unidentified; record chosen visual matches honestly. Generic font substitutions and repeated caption pills remain rejected.

Separate roles: hero emphasis uses a few meaningful words composed into the frame; identifiers use stable name/role hierarchy; accessibility captions accurately carry full speech and may be a separate track. YouTube selectable captions are not evidence of baked type.

For a foreground/background reveal:
1. Pick a shot with negative space and useful silhouette. Design the final text position first.
2. Composite footage → subtle contrast treatment → text → authentic masked foreground person/object. Keep foreground and underlying footage transforms identical.
3. Reveal type from an occluded edge/architectural line, settle, hold, then leave at the next idea. Match the scene's movement when practical.
4. Keep every key letter readable after the reveal; never cover eyes/mouth to make the layout fit. If the head obscures the word, reduce type size, widen the source framing or shift the word into negative space. For Lena v4, entry lasts about0.65s followed by more than2s of clear hold. Treat these as episode choices, not universal timing.
5. Inspect the moving mask for hair shimmer, halos, lag, hand crossings and colour mismatch. Do not alter identity for an easy cutout.

Where masking fails, use a designed reveal in genuine negative space and explicitly distinguish it from a behind-person effect. Never claim an absent effect.

Starting values, adapted per shot: 6–12 entry frames and 4–8 exit frames at 24fps; readable phrase-led hold; small settling movement or restrained blur-to-sharp. Avoid repeated bounce/typewriter/elastic motion and a sound on every word. At 1080p, short hero words may start around 90–150px and support text 32–48px, adjusted to real font proportions. Keep about 5–8% outer clearance plus face/caption space. Review a 360px-wide rendition and actual playback.

## Identifiers and explanatory drawings

Use one matching brand identification treatment for host and guest, each shown once. Follow-up questions carry no repeated host name/role. For object-based explanations, transcript timing, split/full-frame decisions and Nate's actual reference, read [illustrated-explainers.md](illustrated-explainers.md).

## Images, camera and transitions

Every addition serves evidence, explanation, orientation or a truthful emotional association. Illustrations must be labelled when they might look like real emails, detector results or news. Do not invent realistic evidence. Build a visual scene: establish → relevant detail → hold the result. Use parallax, focus, paper layers and annotation only to clarify it. Generic robots/typing/skylines are not substitutes for relevant proof.

Straight cuts are the base. J-cuts introduce the next idea before its image; L-cuts carry a sentence across an authentic reaction/illustration. Phrase-led punch-ins shift emphasis rather than firing at fixed intervals. Keep eye-line coherent and zoom around the person. Crop only within useful genuine resolution. Do not transplant listener reactions to manufacture shock/agreement.

Match cuts, focus transitions and short audio bridges can mark meaningful changes. Avoid transition packs between every soundbite. Let the full conversation become calmer than its trailer.

## Score as an arrangement

Choose musical texture from the actual emotional register: a PR credibility dilemma needs restrained tension, not disaster-movie stakes. Audition licensed/included assets against speech. Save **output start/end | asset/source in/out | emotional job | gain/automation | silence/transition | licence**.

Keep dialogue, score and accents separate. Rebuild from clean tracks to remove old baked music. A remastered inseparable mix is not a new score. Develop purposeful changes: opening texture → complication introduces rhythm/texture → insight thins/changes the harmony → key question gets a dip/silence → resolved handoff into a quieter interview. Do not use a constant loop at 5% or constant crescendo.

Edit music at compatible phrase/bar boundaries where possible. Choose fade lengths from the arrangement. Do not chop sustained notes on every picture cut. Use original/licensed accents sparingly and away from first consonants. Tone-generator pulses alone are not a finished cinematic score.

Mix speech first, then score comfortably below it, automating quiet words and pauses. Gentle ducking can help; inspect pumping. Fix masking through a sparser cue or restrained EQ, not just louder dialogue. Check headphones, mono and small speakers at modest volume.

Review dialogue only, score only, accents only, visuals without audio, then complete sequence. Record which passes actually happened. Meters/transcripts cannot establish emotional fit or intelligibility. −16 LUFS stereo and true peak ≤−1.5dBTP are useful spoken-word starting targets, not a taste score. Measure the delivered encode.

## Conversation and acceptance

Cut repetitions/false starts that obstruct meaning, but keep thinking, uncertainty, emotion, humour and useful pauses. Use selective reaction/angle changes and insert proof where needed. Keep unscored passages when speech holds attention. Match exposure/white balance while preserving skin tones; avoid aggressive teal/orange grading or destructive denoise.

Review the actual opening in motion and at phone size: premise understood; exact meanings retained; depth effect genuinely visible and clean; text readable; score follows the story; quiet words clear; joins intentional; final question paid off; handoff resolved. A beautiful still or clean loudness result is insufficient. Fix defects and replay the affected passage before the full render. Technical checks and perceptual checks must be reported separately.
