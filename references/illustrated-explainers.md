## Scene construction

1. Start with the real transcript and one relationship or process that benefits from a drawing. Write source word anchors for each visible state.
2. Draw recognisable objects: an article, post, document, detector gate, inbox or person. Short text labels orient them. A headline plus three bullet points is not an illustrated explanation.
3. Establish the object, show its relevant action/change, then let the result read. Use measured path drawing, a bounded object entrance and a purposeful connector/state change. Avoid perpetual motion or decorative robots.
4. Use a full-frame drawing for a brief complex relationship; keep the guest beside the drawing during a longer account. Use authentic same-time portrait footage and preserve facial resolution. Neither labels nor connectors cross the face.
5. Time the change to the spoken claim: a rejection stamp appears when the platform verdict is described; a whole-post border/label appears when the label's scope is described; a human connection is drawn when the guest says they will answer personally. Do not announce the result before the actual story reaches it.
6. Distinguish advice, questions and accounts from proven results. Measurement diagrams use questions, not invented leads/conversion numbers. Detector/post scenes remain clearly labelled illustrations. Do not generate realistic client evidence.
7. Review early, middle and held states, motion progression, the portrait split and phone-size readability. Check support labels against line drawings as well as against the background. Keep a quiet hold once an idea is clear.
