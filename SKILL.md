## Decisions controlling the edit

1. Write the viewer promise and a paper edit with exact source in/out times. Build from actual episode lines: immediate tension, understandable context, useful insight, emotional change and a specific unresolved question. Do not manufacture question/answer pairs, reactions or certainty by rearranging speech.
2. Design the **first 60–90 seconds** as a complete sequence when requested. A shorter coherent story beats a padded montage. Preserve explicit duration choices. The dialogue must work without graphics/music first.
3. Design from the footage: negative space, exact licensed font files, deliberate hierarchy, contrast and depth. Use the owner's verified channel identity, including its genuine amber-orange accent when applicable. **No arbitrary palette, repeated black caption pills, generic fonts or corporate graphics.** Brand consistency and fully readable text take priority over a reference show's surface appearance. For AI Cross Fields, use the [brand reference](references/ai-cross-fields-brand.md).
4. Integrate hero text into the scene. Use foreground/background reveal or occlusion for a meaningful moment; inspect moving edges. Settle every hero word into a fully readable hold in clean space after its depth reveal. Brief entry occlusion must not hide central letters throughout the hold. Keep supporting text in clean space. If true masking cannot be rendered reliably, use a designed spatial reveal and disclose the limitation. Never claim an absent effect.
5. Save a music cue sheet: named assets, emotional jobs, entrances, changes, dips, silence and release. A constant stock bed at 5% is insufficient. Mix dialogue first; review score alone, accents alone and combined. No listening claim when only meters/transcripts were available.
6. Identify each speaker once with matching brand treatments. Follow-up questions must not repeatedly reintroduce the host. Illustrate concepts with objects and visible relationships/state changes when useful; more text is not a substitute for a drawing.
7. Keep the interview moving at the pace in [fast-pace-seven-habits.md](references/fast-pace-seven-habits.md), while preserving humour, real reactions and pauses that carry meaning.

## Execution integrity

Preserve originals/compositions and version outputs. Rebuild from clean source when removing baked graphics/music; do not stack new titles over old ones or call re-normalising an inseparable mix a new score. Test the hardest actual typography/mask shot before the full render. Use an appropriate compositor when simple text filters cannot implement the direction.

Verify names, roles, quotes and claim ownership. Label illustrations/reconstructions that could look like actual detector results, emails, news or client evidence. Never invent replacement speech or guest experience. Record source-to-output mappings for rearrangements/repairs.

Use licensed/included assets within the approved ceiling and save licence/attribution evidence. New metered generation needs a task-specific ceiling. No paid tools or AI generation without Yousif's agreed budget. Editing does not authorise publication or messages to the guest.

## Deliver and verify

Create a rendered opening preview before a lengthy full render. A preview does not establish full-episode readiness. If direction is already authorised, continue without asking the same approval again.

Verify the **delivered file**: dimensions, frame count, duration, full decode, unexpected black/frozen spans, speech-preserving joins, loudness/peak headroom, sync across joins and near ending. Inspect detector flags visually. Review typography in motion and at phone size, masks, names, cutaways and ending. Audio meters cannot establish taste/intelligibility or whole-episode listening.

Titles use **"<hook> | <Guest Name> | Ep. N"**, plain text, always with the episode number. Keep paper edit, cue sheet, source map, credits and QA with existing episode state. Distinguish technical checks, actual viewing/listening, missing verification and owner's publishing review. Identify one current master. Failed/older renders are not deliverables. Map feedback to time ranges; retain reusable preferences without making episode-specific choices universal.
